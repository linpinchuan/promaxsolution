Facebook Ads or Google Ads: where should a business start?

By Pro Max Solution Marketing Agency 7 Sep 2026 6 min read

Start with Google Ads if people already search for what you sell. Start with Facebook Ads if your product is new, visual or unknown, and you need to create interest first. Many businesses end up using both, each for a different job.

The core difference

Google Ads captures demand that already exists. Someone types a search, and your ad appears while they are actively looking. Facebook Ads, which also run on Instagram through Meta, create demand. Your ad appears while people scroll, based on their interests and behaviour, before they have decided to look for anything.

Google AdsFacebook Ads
Customer mindsetSearching for a solution nowScrolling, not yet looking
Best forServices and products people already search forNew brands, visual products, offers and lead generation
Main formatsText ads, shopping and remarketingImages, video and instant lead forms
Typical strengthHigh intent, often closer to a saleReach and targeting, lower cost per lead in many cases
Typical riskExpensive clicks in competitive keywordsLeads that look good but do not buy

When to start with Google Ads

  • People already search for your service, for example "emergency plumber" or "accounting software".
  • You need enquiries quickly and can handle them right away.
  • Your offer is easy to explain in a short search ad and a clear landing page.

When to start with Facebook Ads

  • Your product or brand is new, so few people search for it yet.
  • Your offer is visual, such as food, fashion, beauty, property or events.
  • You want to collect leads with an instant form or retarget people who visited your website.

How to run a fair first test

  1. Pick one channel and one clear offer.
  2. Set up conversion tracking before you spend anything, so you can see which ads produce enquiries.
  3. Give the test enough budget and time to collect data, usually a few weeks, before you judge it.
  4. Judge results by cost per lead and lead quality, not clicks or likes.
  5. Keep the winners, stop the losers and test the next idea.

Your landing page matters as much as the ad

A good ad sends people to a page that repeats the promise, shows proof and makes the next step obvious. If the page is slow, confusing or asks for too much, even the best campaign wastes money. Fix the page and the tracking first, then scale the budget.

Using both together

A common pattern is to let Facebook Ads introduce your business to new people, let Google Ads capture those who search for you or your category, and use remarketing on both to bring visitors back. Start with one channel, learn what works, then add the second.
